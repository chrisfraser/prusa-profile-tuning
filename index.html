Dialled In

Take a Prusa MK3S, MK4S, or Core One from out-of-the-box to a print profile you actually trust. Every number in this course is one you measure, on your machine, with your spool — not one you copy. Menu paths and target values are split per printer throughout, because M900 K and M572 S are not the same thing and never will be.

◎ The mission

Three Prusa printers, three materials, one humid island. The goal is repeatable quality at a sane speed: hit print, walk away, get a part that's dimensionally right and doesn't delaminate — and when something does go wrong, be able to name the single setting that caused it. Not maximum speed. Trust.

▲ The one rule that outranks everything

This fleet runs two firmware families. The MK3S is Marlin: Linear Advance, M900 K. The MK4S and Core One are Buddy: Pressure Advance, M572 S. The values live on different scales, the menus live in different places, and copying a number from one to the other will quietly ruin a profile. Every lesson that touches a menu path or a G-code command splits into three panels — open only your machine.

Phase 1

The machine

earn a trustworthy printer before you tune a profile

Every filament calibration measures filament plus machine. If the machine is loose, warm-drifting or out of square, you will spend an evening carefully measuring your printer's faults and writing them into a filament profile. These four lessons close that door. Do them once per printer — and again after any hardware change.

    L5 Selftest and the health check Run your printer's own hardware check and read the result properly. Separate MK3S, MK4S and Core One paths, plus what each test actually proves. Ready L6 Belt tension and squareness MK3S belt-status numbers, MK4S tensioner screws, Core One belt frequencies in Hz. The measurement that has to be right before Input Shaper means anything. Ready L7 First layer and Z-offset PINDA versus loadcell — two genuinely different procedures. Getting a first layer you can judge, and knowing which knob moved it. Ready L8 Thermal and motion trust PID tuning, thermal model calibration, Input Shaper, and the MK3S-only linearity correction. The last things to fix before the machine stops being a variable. Ready
Phase 2

The filament

import a baseline, then seven steps in dependency order

Now the machine is a constant and the filament is the variable. First import the starter presets so every number has somewhere to live — then seven measurements, always in this order, because each one assumes the ones before it are fixed. Re-run an early step and every later number is void. One legitimate exception, in L16.

    L9 Import the starter profiles Download the eSun bundle — six presets, two materials × three printers — import it, and know exactly how much to trust it: every value inherited, none measured. Yet. Ready L10 The temperature tower Find the lowest temperature that still bonds layers. Custom G-code per band, and the snap test that tells you the truth. Ready L11 Extrusion multiplier Single-wall box, twelve caliper readings, one formula. Plus the M221 trap that makes people correct the same error twice. Ready L12 Pressure / Linear Advance The single biggest quality win — and the one place where MK3S and MK4S numbers must never touch. Tuned at production speed or not at all. Ready L13 Retraction Rule out the other three causes of stringing first. Then spike towers, filament overrides, and why more is not a free win on direct drive. Ready L14 Cooling Not one fan speed — two time thresholds, an interpolation and a speed brake. Fan bands per material, and what an enclosure changes. Ready L15 Max volumetric speed Your real speed limit. Find where the wall goes matte, take 85 % of it, and read the flow-rate preview to see it working. Ready L16 Speeds and accelerations Only now. External perimeters low, travel high, dynamic overhang speed on — then sign Phase 2 off. Ready
Phase 3

Ship it

turn measurements into profiles you keep

Measurements that live in a notebook are lost. These lessons turn them into named, machine-scoped, backed-up PrusaSlicer profiles — and a log that keeps a three-printer fleet honest. Each of Phase 2's seven numbers now has somewhere to live, a test that says whether it worked, and a record that says whether you measured it or inherited it.

    L17 Profile structure and naming One profile per printer family, compatible-printer conditions that make a mis-selection impossible, and never overwriting a system profile. Ready L18 The validation set Three prints and five acceptance criteria — each failure naming the step to go back to. Sign-off, with a scope. Ready L19 The tuning log Per spool, per printer, per date — and every value marked measured or inherited. The habit that makes the other eighteen lessons compound. Ready

⌁ How this works

Work through the lessons in order — each is short and ends with a task you paste back to me for feedback. I'm your tutor: run Claude Code in this folder and say "I'm on Lesson 1", ask anything, or paste your measurements for a line-by-line critique. Stuck, curious, or want to jump ahead? Just say so. Already fluent in the fundamentals? Each Phase 0 lesson has a skip straight to Phase 1 link at the top.

Mark a lesson complete at the bottom of its page and the bar above will pick up where you left off. Four lessons carry calculators you feed your own measurements to, and checklist ticks survive a reload so a sign-off can be worked through at the printer. All of it is stored in this browser only — no account, nothing uploaded.
